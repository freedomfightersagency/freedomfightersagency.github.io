Family protection, explained clearly

Life insurance is about
protecting the people you love.

If your income helps keep your household running, it’s worth asking how your family would manage financially if you were no longer here. Life insurance can provide a financial benefit to the people you choose, subject to the policy terms.

01 / Term life insurance

Coverage for a chosen period of time.

How it generally works

Term life insurance provides a death benefit if the insured person dies during the policy term and the policy is in force. Terms commonly run for a set number of years. Premiums, renewability, conversion rights, exclusions, and other features depend on the specific policy.

Who may consider it?

People who want to consider financial protection during years when responsibilities may be higher—such as raising children, paying a mortgage, or replacing household income. Whether it fits depends on your needs and budget.

Important: Term coverage does not automatically build cash value. If the insured outlives the term, coverage generally ends unless the contract provides a renewal or conversion option and the policyholder exercises it.
02 / Mortgage protection

Help your family keep more options open.

Mortgage protection is a common reason homeowners explore life insurance. The goal is to consider whether a life insurance benefit could help loved ones manage the mortgage and other household expenses if the insured dies.

Life insurance used for mortgage needs

With an individually owned life insurance policy, the policy owner generally names the beneficiary. If a covered death occurs while the policy is active, the beneficiary receives the death benefit and may use it toward the mortgage or other needs. The benefit is not automatically paid to the lender unless the policy arrangement says otherwise.

Not the same as mortgage insurance

Mortgage life insurance offered through a lender can work differently from an individual life insurance policy. Mortgage insurance such as PMI is also different: it generally protects the lender against certain loan losses, not the homeowner’s family with a life insurance death benefit.

03 / What affects coverage?

There is no one-size-fits-all number.

Household needs

Mortgage balance, income replacement, childcare, education, debts, and final expenses can all be part of the conversation.

Budget

Premiums depend on factors such as age, health, coverage amount, term length, tobacco use, and underwriting.

Policy details

Review exclusions, riders, conversion options, renewal costs, beneficiary designations, and what happens at the end of the term.

Common questions

Good questions to ask before applying.

How much life insurance do I need?

There is no universal amount. Consider debts, mortgage obligations, income replacement, dependents, savings, existing coverage, and what your household could realistically afford. A licensed professional can help you evaluate options, but the decision should reflect your circumstances.

Does mortgage protection pay off my mortgage automatically?

Not necessarily. With an individually owned life policy, the named beneficiary typically receives the death benefit and decides how to use it, subject to the contract. Other mortgage-related products may have different structures.

Is term life always better than whole life?

No. They are designed differently. Term life provides coverage for a specified period and generally has lower initial premiums. Whole life is a type of permanent insurance with guaranteed elements defined by the contract and typically higher premiums. The right choice depends on goals, budget, and policy details.

Can I qualify if I have health conditions?

Possibly. Eligibility and pricing depend on the insurer, the condition, medical history, and underwriting. Only an insurer can determine an application outcome.

Does requesting information mean I have to buy?

No. An educational conversation or quote discussion does not obligate you to purchase coverage. Review the policy and application information carefully before making a decision.
